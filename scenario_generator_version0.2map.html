Welcome to the scenario library

Scenario exercises enable participants to envision and analyze the future through detailed stories of how the future might unfold. Scenarios were popularized in military operations in the 1950s, and are now used routinely in emergency management, governments, nonprofits, and corporations.

Most commonly, scenarios present an extreme or hypothetical situation that tests how people collaborate across organizations and handle emergencies. Major outcomes include collaboration, learning, development of new skills, and improvements to plans and protocols.

This tool will generate customized disaster scenarios based on real scientific data. You will get to personalize each scenario based on your local situation and interests.

This is a demonstration tool. We are excited to receive your feedback.

After the ten days

The scenario you have explored covers the first ten days of the event. This section invites you to look further — at longer-term impacts, recovery needs, and what this experience suggests for future preparedness.

What are the expected long-term impacts of this event, and how should they be managed?

Consider: which communities or systems will take the longest to recover? What secondary impacts — on health, livelihoods, and social cohesion — are likely to emerge in the weeks and months ahead?

What forms of capacity are required for recovery and development beyond the emergency phase?

Consider: which actors, resources, and relationships are most important for recovery? What capacity was depleted during the response that now needs to be restored or rebuilt?

How can lessons drawn from the system scenarios explored in this exercise inform longer-term strategic planning?

Consider: what structural changes — in coordination, resource allocation, or community relationships — would make the response to a similar event more effective? What would need to change before the next event occurs?
